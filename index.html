Manifesto

There is something obscene in the way we consume war now.

It arrives not with the weight of history or the stench of blood,
but as a fleeting image on a screen.

Compressed between an influencer's breakfast
and an ad for the latest electric car.

The crying child in a cratered street.
The severed limb in the rubble.
The dust cloud hanging like a ghost.

All of it is real.
The camera does not lie.
But the camera cannot speak.

What it captures is stripped of its context.
Violence, without memory.
Suffering, without history.

The viewer scrolls on.

The missile strike dissolves into a meme.
The tragedy fades behind a tap.

We are living through a collapse of moral proportion.
War and comedy. Atrocity and lifestyle tips.
Carnage and celebrity news.

All of it delivered in the same feed.
All of it treated the same.

Even the legacy media now trades in euphemism.
"Surgical intervention."
"Kinetic action."
"Targeted neutralization."

Words chosen not to disturb.
Not to offend. Not to feel.

This detachment is not neutral.
It shapes opinion. It shields us from guilt.
It makes war seem manageable.

A war that is not felt is a war that can be ignored.

The tragedy is real for those who endure it.
But unreal for those who consume it.
Filtered. Softened. Suspended between spectacle and fact.

In my work, I try to tear through that veil.
Not to show war, but to make it present.
Not to trigger emotion, but to restore agency.

Because war has many dimensions.
And unless we find ways to feel what we cannot see,
we become numb to the horrors,
untouched by the very catastrophes we allow to continue.

In 36,000
Ways

2022 — Ongoing

A single fragmentation weapon produces 36,000 shards upon detonation. Each one travels at the speed of sound. Each one is designed to kill.

This installation begins with a single piece of shrapnel collected from the front lines of southern Ukraine. You hold it. Nothing else happens until your imagination does.

IDFA DocLab 2025 Gallery Anahita Sadighi Berlin 2024 TecArt Rotterdam 2026 Max Planck Institute 2024
The
Enemy

003

The
Enemy

2013 — 2018

A multi-user VR installation placing audiences face to face with combatants from opposite sides of three conflicts: Israel/Palestine, the Congo, El Salvador.

Developed with Professor Fox Harrell at MIT, the work uses AI-driven interaction to track each visitor's biases in real time and respond. Not a film. Not a game. A confrontation.

Rose d'Or 2017 Numix Grand Prix 2018 World VR Forum 2018 Sundance 2015 MIT Museum Institut du Monde Arabe
Seven
Grams

002

Seven
Grams

2018 — 2022

An augmented reality experience that turns your smartphone into the instrument of its own indictment. Seven grams of rare earth minerals. Every phone. Every hand.

The work reveals the human cost of mineral extraction in the Democratic Republic of Congo, made visible through the object that depends on it.

Sundance 2022 Crystal Owl Award 2022 FIPADOC Best Digital Storytelling Prix Europa Honorable Mention

1999 — 2012

From
the
Field

For fifteen years, working for Time, Vanity Fair, Le Monde, Le Monde 2, Stern and GEO, I photographed conflict across more than 90 countries. Kosovo. Gaza. Afghanistan. Somalia. The West Bank. Kashmir. Yemen.

I was there. I saw it. I documented it with precision and care.

Then I stopped. Because I became convinced that images of conflict don't change the people who see them, nor the lives of the people I photographed.

About

Karim Ben Khelifa

Karim Ben Khelifa is a Belgian-Tunisian artist whose practice engineers confrontation with armed conflict through virtual reality, spatial sound, kinetic sculpture, and installation.

Drawing on two decades as a frontline photojournalist, he abandoned documentation when he became convinced that images of conflict don't change the people who see them, nor the lives of the people he photographed. The question that followed him out of photojournalism still drives everything he makes: if documentation fails, what doesn't?

His answer has been consistent across five major works: stop representing war. Start engineering the conditions under which a person cannot avoid reckoning with it on their own terms.

He is the founder of Atelier Non Fiction, a Berlin-based boutique production company.
